# Hiragana Buddy — Vocabulary Spelling Mini Game

Nov 27, 2026 · @Liz

## Overview and goals

Hiragana Buddy gets a third mini game: the child sees a picture, hears its Japanese word, and spells the word by putting kana tiles into slots. The words are the class vocabulary lists, which use one, two or three hiragana from the class kana sets. One game is 8 words and ends with 1 to 3 stars.

Listen & Match teaches one sound for one kana. This game is the next step: several sounds in order make a word that means something. It uses the same pebble tray, stepping-stone path, stars and audio as Listen & Match, so a child who knows one game already knows how the other works.

**Goals**

- A pre-reader can play a whole game alone, with no text to read.

- The child hears every word broken into its sounds and then blended back together.

- Every game ends with at least one star, and nothing marks a mistake as a failure.

- The words, their order and their meanings match the class worksheets.

**Success looks like**

- In playtests, children aged 5–6 finish an 8-word game in about 3–5 minutes without asking what to do.

- The number of words spelled with no wrong tiles goes up when a child repeats a stage.

- A native speaker approves every word clip, including the pitch of the two-meaning words.

**Out of scope for v1:** words longer than three kana, katakana words, typing or handwriting the word, timers, and links into the writing game.

## How a game plays

### Getting in

The home screen gets a fourth main button with a picture icon, and the four buttons sit in a 2 × 2 grid. The button opens the game's own stepping-stone path, with one stone per stage: 1A, 1B, 2A, 2B. The mascot stands on the child's current stage, and one tap on that stone starts the game. This path does not depend on Listen & Match progress.

### One word, step by step

A game is the stage's 8 words in a shuffled order.

1.  **Prompt.** The word's picture appears and the mascot says the word. Tapping the mascot or the picture says it again, and that never costs anything.

2.  **Slots.** Under the picture is one empty slot per kana in the word.

3.  **Tiles.** Below the slots are the word's kana, one tile per letter, plus one extra tile. The tile order is shuffled. A word has 2 to 4 tiles on screen.

4.  **Tap.** Tapping a tile sends it to the leftmost empty slot, if it is the right kana for that slot.

5.  **Drag.** The child can also drag a tile into any empty slot. It stays if it is the right kana for that slot, even when earlier slots are still empty.

6.  **Right tile.** The tile lands in its slot and says its sound. It leaves a gap where it was, and the other tiles don't move.

7.  **Wrong tile.** The tile wiggles with a gentle "boop", slides back, and the word is said again. There is no red X and no buzzer.

8.  **Third wrong tile on one word.** The tile for the leftmost empty slot glows, so no child gets stuck.

9.  **Word finished.** Each tile says its sound from left to right. Then the whole word is spoken, the picture bounces, and a spray of stars flies out. A pebble lands in the tray and the next word starts by itself.

### Tile rules

- **One tile per letter.** みみ shows two み tiles and とまと shows two と tiles. Two tiles with the same kana are interchangeable, and either one fits either slot for that kana.

- **The extra tile** is picked at random from the 8 kana of the stage's own class set, and is never a kana that is in the word. For a set 2 word such as くま, the extra tile is a set 2 kana.

- **What counts as a wrong tile:** a tap on a tile that isn't the next letter, or a drag into a slot that needs a different kana. A drag dropped outside any slot slides back and counts as nothing.

### はし, the two-picture word

Both pictures show side by side. The audio says はし for the bridge while the bridge lights up, then はし for the chopsticks while the chopsticks light up. The child spells the word once. When the word is finished, both readings play again in the same way.

### Designing for pre-readers

- A short practice word with the mascot plays on the first visit and isn't scored. A hand shows the two moves: tap a tile, or drag it to a slot.

- Tiles and slots are at least 2 cm square with 1 cm gaps, and tiles draw each kana from the writing game's stroke data.

- Taps and drags are accepted only after the word has finished playing.

- No timers, no lives, and no game over. A small home button sits in a top corner, and a game left unfinished isn't scored.

- With reduced motion switched on, the bounce and the star spray become a short fade.

## Scoring, progress and rewards

### Stars

A word is a **clean word** when the child spells it with no wrong tile. A word that needed the glowing hint has already had wrong tiles, so it isn't clean. Replays of the word never count against the child. Stars depend on how many of the 8 words were clean:

| Clean words (of 8) | Stars |
|--------------------|-------|
| 7–8                | 3     |
| 5–6                | 2     |
| 0–4                | 1     |

These are the same thresholds as Listen & Match, and they live in one config value so playtests can tune them.

A child tapping at random gets about 2.7 clean words in stage 1A, 1.7 in 2A, 1.3 in 1B and 1.1 in 2B. Stage 1A is the easiest to score on because its four one-kana words show only two tiles each. Two stars still take 5 clean words, which is above random tapping in every stage.

### Progress the child can see

| Level      | What the child sees                                                                               | When it changes |
|------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------|
| This game  | The pebble tray fills left to right: a gold pebble for a clean word, a plain pebble for any other | Every word      |
| The stages | The mascot moves along the path, and each stone shows its best stars                              | After each game |

Each stone shows the picture of the stage's first word (the hand for 1A) and the best stars earned on it. *(Changed Nov 2026: no empty star outlines anywhere in the app, only the stars earned.)*

### Unlocking

- Stage 1A is open from the start.

- A stage opens when the stage before it reaches 3 stars. Stages behind the mascot stay open for replay.

- A stage that isn't open isn't shown: the path ends at the last open stone, and the next stone appears when it opens. *(Changed Nov 2026.)*

- The parent-gated settings get an Unlock whole path switch for this path. Turning it off closes the unearned stages again and keeps every star.

- Progress is kept per profile, and a replay with fewer stars never lowers the best result.

### Rewards

- **Each tile:** the kana says its sound as it lands.

- **Each word:** the sounds play one by one, the word is spoken, the picture bounces, and stars spray out. Every word gets this, clean or not.

- **End of game:** the same results sequence as Listen & Match. The full tray is cheered first, the gold pebbles count up into stars, and two picture buttons offer play again or go on along the path.

- **On the path:** new best stars fly onto the stone. When a stage first reaches 3 stars, the next stone appears on the path and the mascot hops onto it.

The game never uses streaks, daily rewards, countdowns, coins, shops or surprise prizes.

## Audio and pictures

### Word audio

The game needs 33 word clips: one per word, and two for はし. The single-kana sounds that play as tiles land are the same clips Listen & Match uses.

- **Voice for now:** VOICEVOX with the 四国めたん voice, generated by us, with the credit "VOICEVOX:四国めたん" on the About screen. A native-speaker recording replaces the clips later under the same file names.

- **One-kana words** (て, き, め, か, は) are spoken as words. は must be "ha", not the particle "wa".

- **Processing** follows the Listen & Match spec: trimmed to about 30 ms of silence at each end, loudness matched to the kana clips, mono, exported as .mp3.

### Pitch for words with two meanings

In standard Tokyo Japanese these words change meaning with pitch, so each clip must match its picture. A text-to-speech voice can pick the wrong one, so the accent is set by hand for these clips.

| Word | Picture    | Pitch          |
|------|------------|----------------|
| はし | bridge     | low, then high |
| はし | chopsticks | high, then low |
| あめ | rain       | high, then low |
| かき | persimmon  | low, then high |
| した | down       | low, then high |

This table is from general knowledge of standard pitch accent. A native speaker should confirm it and listen to all 33 clips on the audio review page before release.

### Pictures

The game needs 33 pictures: one per word, and two for はし. We draw them ourselves, so the app owns them. The clip art on the class worksheets is not copied.

- **Style:** the pebble-garden look of the rest of the app, with thick dark outlines, flat colours from the app palette, and no text.

- **Format:** SVG, one file per picture, named by word (tsuki.svg, hashi-bridge.svg, hashi-chopsticks.svg). *(Nov 2026: the art came as picture sheets; scripts/cut-vocab-art.py cuts them into transparent WebP files with these names, tsuki.webp and so on.)*

- **Readable small:** each picture must be recognizable at about 3 cm across, because it also appears on a stepping stone.

- **Pictures that need care:** かた (a shoulder, marked so it doesn't read as an arm), みて (a child pointing and looking), つきみ (a full moon with dango), した (an arrow pointing down), and か (a mosquito that doesn't read as a fly).

## Implementation plan

The game is one more screen in the existing static TypeScript app, with no backend. It reuses Listen & Match's audio, tray, path, results and scoring code, and adds a word list, a spelling state machine, drag handling and one IndexedDB store.

| vocab/stages.ts                  | New           | Loads the word list, builds the stages, and checks them: 8 words per stage, 1 to 3 full-size kana per word, every kana in the allowed sets.      |

### Game logic

- The extra tile is drawn from the stage's class set kana, minus the kana in the word. Tile order is shuffled per word.

- A tap places a tile only in the leftmost empty slot. A drag places a tile in the slot it is dropped on. Either way the tile stays only if its kana equals that slot's kana.

- Tiles with the same kana are interchangeable.

- Each wrong tile adds one to the word's count. At three, the tile for the leftmost empty slot glows.

- A word is clean when its wrong-tile count is zero.

- Input is locked while the prompt or the finish sequence plays. A touch during the lock is ignored and not counted.

- Drag uses the writing game's touch rules: one active pointer, palm rejection, and pointercancel returns the tile without a wrong-tile count.

- A stage is unlocked when the stage before it has a best result of 3 stars, or the parent switch is on.

### Testing

- **Unit tests:** word-list validation, the extra-tile rule, duplicate-kana tiles (みみ, とまと), clean-word scoring, and the unlock rule.

- **State machine tests** with scripted input: all clean, tap out of order, drag into a later slot, three wrong tiles then the hint, and touches during a lock.

- **Playwright:** spell つき by tap and by drag, and check the finish sequence plays kana, kana, then the word.

- **Device checks** on iPad Safari, an iPhone and an Android phone: drag feels direct under a small finger, and audio sequences play without gaps.

- **Playtests:** 3–5 children with a parent present. Watch whether they tap or drag, whether the two はし pictures confuse them, and how many games each stage takes to reach 3 stars.

### Milestones

1.  **Word data and audio:** the word list for stages 1A to 2B, 33 interim word clips with hand-set pitch, and the review page extended to words.

2.  **Playable prototype:** stage 1A with placeholder pictures, tap placement, the finish sequence and the results screen.

3.  **Full rules:** drag placement, the hint, はし with two pictures, all four stages, and the vocabResults store with backup and restore.

4.  **Path and home:** the stage path with unlocks and the parent switch, the fourth home button, and the practice word.

5.  **Pictures and polish:** the 33 final pictures, the star spray, sound effects and reduced motion.

6.  **Playtest and tune**, then release to families.

**Decided 2026-11-27:** the prompt is the picture plus the spoken word. Tiles are one per letter plus one extra tile from the set, never a kana in the word. Taps fill the leftmost slot, and drags are accepted in any correct slot. はし shows both pictures and plays both readings. A game is 8 words, and each set is two stages in worksheet order. **Risks**

- **One-kana words can be guessed.** て, き, め, か and は show two tiles, so a guess is right half the time. Mitigation: the star thresholds sit above random tapping, and playtests check whether stage 1A is too easy to pass.

- **The voice says the wrong pitch.** A text-to-speech clip could say "candy" for the rain picture. Mitigation: accents are set by hand for the listed words, and a native speaker reviews every clip.

- **Dragging is hard for small fingers.** Mitigation: tapping always works, drop targets are generous, and a drag that misses a slot costs nothing.

- **A child stalls on a stage.** Three stars takes 7 clean words of 8, which is harder in the B stages with their three-kana words. Mitigation: the hint, the parent switch, and a count of games per stage in playtests.

- **A picture is misread.** A child may see "arm" for かた or "boy" for みて. Mitigation: the word is always spoken with the picture, and playtests note any picture children name wrongly.
